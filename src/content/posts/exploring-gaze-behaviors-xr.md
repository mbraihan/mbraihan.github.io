## 1. Introduction

Gaze is a powerful social signal that can support coordination and communication in collaborative settings. In XR, where physical co-presence cues are limited, understanding gaze behaviors is essential for designing effective collaborative tools. In this study, we investigate how gaze is used during collaborative XR design tasks and how it relates to team performance.

## 2. Study Design

### 2.1 Participants
We recruited 24 participants (12 pairs) with prior experience in XR or 3D modeling. All pairs were unfamiliar with each other.

### 2.2 Tasks & Procedure
Participants collaborated in pairs to complete three design tasks in a shared XR environment. We recorded gaze data using a head-mounted eye tracker and logged in-app events.

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Gaze Communication
Teams frequently used gaze to initiate and maintain communication. Mutual gaze and gaze shifts preceded verbal turns in 68% of cases.

### 3.2 Attention Coordination
We observed high alignment in attention during joint planning phases, with a mean attention overlap of 0.62 (SD = 0.14).

### 3.3 Object Referencing
Gaze was used to reference objects in 74% of all references, often accompanied by deictic gestures.

## 4. Discussion

Our findings highlight the role of gaze as a lightweight yet effective communication channel in collaborative XR. Designers should consider gaze-aware features to support coordination and reduce miscommunication.

## 5. Takeaways

- Gaze behaviors strongly correlate with communication and coordination.
- Supporting gaze awareness can improve team performance in XR.
- Future tools should integrate gaze cues in a privacy-aware manner.

## 6. Future Work

We plan to explore long-term collaboration, multi-team settings, and interventions that provide augmented gaze feedback to enhance coordination.
