abstract: >-
  Electroencephalography (EEG) captures brain signals from the scalp and has applications in medicine, psychology, rehabilitation, and many other areas. EEG signals are inherently noise-prone, however, and patterns are rarely visible in raw signals. This chapter presents an overview of how computational intelligence is used to discover patterns in brain signals throughout a typical BCI pipeline: preprocessing, feature extraction, feature selection, and classification. Because EEG is the outcome of a complex, nonlinear, non-stationary stochastic biological process with many internal and external noise sources, appropriate computational intelligence methods are required at each step and can significantly improve end results.
